# 10. Defending your proposal

Most Departments require a presentation and questions before the proposal is approved. Confirm the format with your supervisor: duration, audience, whether the panel has read the document.

## The presentation

- **Ten to fifteen minutes** is typical. One slide per minute, at most.
- **Structure follows the logical chain**: context (1 slide), gap (1), question and objectives (1), method (2 to 3), validation plan (1), preliminary results (1 to 2), timeline and risks (1), summary (1).
- **The gap slide is the one that matters.** If the panel leaves understanding exactly what is missing and why it matters, you have done your job.
- **Show, do not tell.** One verified preliminary result is worth three slides of method description.
- **Rehearse to time** in front of lab members. The CERG-FLUX Lab (Fluids, Learning and Uncertainty in compleX systems) Discord is there for this; ask.

## Questions you will be asked

Prepare an answer to each. If you cannot answer one, that is the gap in your preparation, not a reason to hope it is not asked.

- Why this method and not [the obvious alternative]?
- What is the contribution if everything works? What is it if the main hypothesis is wrong?
- How will you know your code is correct?
- What experimental data will you validate against, and what is its uncertainty?
- What happens if [key resource] is not available?
- What is the smallest version of this project that would still be a degree?
- Which of your objectives is the riskiest, and why?
- What have you already done?
- How does this differ from [the closest paper]?
- Why are you the right person to do this?

## Handling questions

- Listen to the whole question. Pause. Answer the question asked, then stop.
- "I do not know, but here is how I would find out" is a strong answer. Bluffing is a weak one and the panel will know.
- If a question reveals a real weakness, say so: "That is a limitation; I propose to address it by...". Then write it down. The panel's questions are free review.
- Disagreement is allowed if you have the evidence. Defend the proposal; do not defend your ego.

## After the defence

- Write up the panel's comments within 48 hours while they are fresh.
- Agree with your supervisor which comments change the proposal and which change the plan.
- Update the repository, tag the approved version, and treat it as the baseline for your annual progress reports.
